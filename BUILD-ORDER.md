# Build order

Seven hours a week. This is the order that gets the business earning soonest,
not the order that finishes the site soonest. Those are different, and the
difference matters more than anything on this list.

## Gate: before anything ships

**Register the entity.** A site that converts and cannot invoice loses the deal
at procurement, which is the most expensive place to lose one. This is not
admin to be done in parallel. It is the gate.

Nothing below is worth doing until an invoice can legally go out.

---

## Week 1 and 2: the free doors, live

Roughly 14 hours.

Ship in this order, because this is the order that starts generating something:

1. **The Leak Test** (`/leak-test`). Already built and scored. Publish it, then
   put the link in your email signature, your LinkedIn bio and the Brief.
   It is the only asset here that works while you sleep.
2. **The One-Move Strategy** (`/one-move`). Highest return per hour on this
   entire list. Each one costs under an hour and produces a person who has seen
   you think about their business before paying you anything. It is also where
   your anonymised case studies will come from, so start the log with the first
   one.
3. **The 20-Minute Leak Call** (`/leak-call`). Last of the three, because it is
   the one that consumes your time rather than producing an asset.

Do not wait for the paid pages to be perfect. A visitor who takes the Test and
gets a real result is worth more than a polished pricing page nobody reached.

**Also this fortnight, because it costs 20 minutes:** put analytics on the site.
You sell conversion architecture. Not knowing where your own visitors drop is a
position you cannot defend on a call.

## Week 3: the paid rungs

Roughly 7 hours.

`/leak-map` and `/conversion-system` are written and live. This week is for the
thing behind them: build the Map template. The document a client receives has to
exist before anyone buys one, and building it now means the first sale does not
turn into a weekend.

Work from the four stages on the Method page. One page per stage, a leak table,
a ranked fix list.

## Week 4: the first long article

Roughly 7 hours.

`The First Hour Is the Whole Funnel` is written and ready to publish. Use this
week to publish it properly rather than to write another: post it, send it to
the Brief, and put it in front of ten people who have the problem it describes.

Then set the rhythm: **one long piece a month, without exception.** Missing a
month costs more than any bad page on this site ever will, because in this model
the blog is not marketing for the business. It is the proof, the lead source and
the sales argument.

## Month 2 onward: the rhythm

Per week, roughly:

- 3 hours: writing. One long piece a month, two to four short ones.
- 2 hours: One-Move Strategy responses. Cap is ten a month and the cap is real.
- 2 hours: delivery and follow-up.

The two short articles for month one are already written and scheduled behind
the long one.

## When there is spare capacity

In this order:

1. **Turn the first three One-Move responses into anonymised outcomes.** Sector,
   situation, intervention, shift. No names. This is the proof layer the site
   currently does not have, and it is the only one that has to be earned rather
   than written.
2. **Point the ad landing pages at something.** The ten sector pages are rebuilt
   and unlisted. They are inert until there is spend behind them, so they wait
   until there is.
3. **Replace the mailto forms with a real endpoint.** Every form on the site
   composes an email today, which works and costs nothing. When volume justifies
   it, swap the handler in `assets/site.js` and nothing else has to change.
4. **The portrait.** Removed. The About page no longer carries a portrait.
   Until it exists the frame shows a labelled placeholder, which is honest. A
   stock photograph here would undo the entire proof argument.

## What not to do

- Do not add a team page.
- Do not put a countdown on a corporate page. Capacity is stated as fact in one
  place, `CAPACITY` in the generator, and every page follows it.
- Do not add case studies you cannot substantiate. One invented proof point
  found is every real one lost.
- Do not lead with content as a product. It sits inside the retainer, after a
  Map has shown it is what closes the gap.
