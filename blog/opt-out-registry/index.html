The Opt-Out Registry Is Coming. Can You Prove Who Asked You to Call?

Published 9 October 20266 min read Novapex

South Africa's National Opt-Out Registry is live. Direct marketers must register by December 2026, the law is enforced in full from 15 April 2027, and consumers can block marketing from May 2027. Under draft guidelines, a block cancels consent you already hold. Contact lists with no record of who asked, when and for what will shrink.

Most businesses have a list they think of as "our leads". Thousands of numbers and email addresses. Some came from inquiries. Some came from a competition three years ago. Some came from a spreadsheet nobody can explain.

From next year, that difference starts to matter. Not because of a new marketing trend. Because of a registry.

What is the National Opt-Out Registry, and when does it start?

It is a national list run by the National Consumer Commission (NCC) where South Africans can block direct marketing. Direct marketers must register and check their contact lists against it before they market to anyone.

The right to block direct marketing has been in the Consumer Protection Act since 2011. It was not enforced in practice. That is now changing, on this timeline:

DateWhat happens
15 September 2026Registration for direct marketers opened
2 October 2026Draft compliance guidelines published
17 October 2026Public comment on the draft guidelines closes
December 2026Deadline for direct marketers to register
December 2026 to April 2027Free cleansing of lists against the registry
15 April 2027The NCC enforces the law in full
May 2027Consumers can start blocking direct marketers

Registration costs R2,574 in 2026, with an annual renewal of R1,930.50. Penalties go up to R1 million or 10% of annual turnover, whichever is greater. TechCentral reported these figures on 8 October 2026.

What happens to the consent you already have?

Under the NCC's draft guidelines, historical or existing consent is invalid once a consumer registers a block. It does not matter when that consent was given.

Read that again with your own list in mind. A tick box from 2023 does not protect you from a block made in 2027. And the Information Regulator has already made clear that an opt-out is not consent. Someone who never replied "STOP" has not said yes.

There is also a running cost. Marketers must clean their lists against the registry monthly and before any campaign. TechCentral reports a cleansing fee of 14 cents per data entry in 2027, rising to 18 cents by 2029. Law firm ENS warns the wording may mean the whole database is charged each time it is cleaned. The NCC has been asked to clarify.

Here is a simple example. The figures are illustrative. If the whole list is charged, a 50,000-contact list cleaned monthly at 14 cents costs R7,000 a month. A list padded with people who never asked to hear from you now costs money just to keep.

Why is this a Capture problem, not a legal problem?

Because the registry does not change who wants to hear from you. It changes how much an unexplained number is worth.

The registry targets direct marketing. Reports so far say purpose decides what counts. BusinessTech, summarising the draft guidelines, notes that a call to arrange a delivery or resolve a complaint is not automatically direct marketing, but an unsolicited sales pitch added to that call is.

So the question for every contact on your list becomes: why is this person here? If you can answer "they asked about our pricing on 3 March, on WhatsApp, about a family plan", you have a real lead and a reason to talk. If the answer is "no idea", you have a cost and a risk.

A number is not a lead. A number with a reason, a date and a yes is.

The pressure behind this is real. Truecaller data cited by the NCC counted 17.5 billion spam calls in South Africa in the first half of 2026, up 25.2% year on year. Buyers are tired of being chased by people they never asked to hear from. The businesses that win their attention will be the ones whose follow-up is plainly wanted.

What should you record when an inquiry comes in?

Five things, at the moment the inquiry arrives. Not later, when someone is cleaning a spreadsheet and guessing.

RecordExampleWhy it matters
SourceWebsite form, WhatsApp, walk-inShows the person came to you
Date9 October 2026Tells you how fresh the interest is
What they askedPrice of a 12-month membershipGives every follow-up a real reason
What they agreed to"Yes, send me offers by email"Separates a reply from marketing consent
ChannelEmail onlyThe registry allows blocks per channel

None of this needs new software. A form field, a column in the CRM, or a line in a WhatsApp label will do. What matters is that it is captured every time, by everyone.

How do you get your list ready before December?

You need an afternoon and an honest look at your list.

1. Split it. Separate people who inquired or bought from people who came from anywhere else: bought lists, old competitions, imported spreadsheets.

2. Fix the form. Add a clear, unticked option for marketing on every inquiry form, separate from the reply they asked for. Say which channel it covers.

3. Record the five things. Start today, for every new inquiry. You cannot rebuild old records, but you can stop creating new blind ones.

4. Trim the dead weight. Contacts with no source, no date and no reply in years are unlikely to buy and may soon cost money to keep. Archive them out of your marketing list.

5. Honour opt-outs fast. When someone asks to stop, remove them across every channel the same day. Confirm it in writing.

If you send marketing at volume, speak to a lawyer about registration. This article explains the change. It is not legal advice, and the guidelines are still drafts.

Where does this fit in the bigger picture?

This sits at the Capture stage of our method. Capture asks one question: when attention turns into an inquiry, do you record it in a way that lets you act on it?

The registry raises the stakes on that question. A pipeline built from real inquiries, each with a reason and a date, keeps its value. A pile of numbers you cannot explain will shrink, and cost you to clean while it does.

Questions people are asking

When can South Africans start blocking direct marketers on the opt-out registry?

From May 2027, according to the National Consumer Commission. Direct marketers must register by December 2026, and the commission will enforce the law in full from 15 April 2027.

Does the opt-out registry cancel consent customers already gave?

Under the NCC's draft compliance guidelines, historical or existing consent is invalid once a consumer registers a pre-emptive block, regardless of when the consent was given.

How much does it cost to register as a direct marketer in South Africa?

The regulations set a 2026 registration fee of R2,574 and an annual renewal fee of R1,930.50. Cleansing a list against the registry is reported at 14 cents per data entry in 2027, rising to 18 cents by 2029.

Is replying to a customer inquiry direct marketing?

Purpose decides. Reports on the draft guidelines say a call to arrange delivery or resolve a complaint is not automatically direct marketing, but an unsolicited sales pitch added to it is. Record what each person asked for, and get legal advice for your case.
