Black Friday Is Not Scored on Friday. Here Is How to Tell What Your Marketing Brought In.

Published 27 November 20266 min read Novapex

To tell what your Black Friday marketing brought in, count four numbers: enquiries, replies, new customers and money in. Compare them with a normal week, not with your likes. Then keep counting into December, because many people look online first and buy later, in store or on WhatsApp.

You posted every day this week. You may have boosted a few posts. The likes came in. And on Monday, someone will ask the question that hurts: what did all of that actually bring in?

If you cannot answer it, you are not alone. Most marketing is judged by the wrong numbers, on the wrong day.

Why can't I tell if my Black Friday marketing worked?

Because the numbers you can see easily are not the numbers that pay you. Likes, views and reach are easy to find. Enquiries, customers and money in take a little work to count, so most businesses never count them.

There is a second problem. The sale often does not happen where the ad was seen. Daily Maverick reported on 26 November that TFG saw about 30% more customers browse online before shopping in store during the first half of its 2027 financial year. So someone sees your post on Facebook, thinks about it, and walks into your shop on Saturday. Your page shows a like. Your till shows a sale. Nothing connects the two unless you connect them.

Black Friday is not a day. It is a door. You only find out who walked through it in December.

How long after Black Friday should you measure results?

At least until mid-December, and ideally into January. Riana Prins, dean of undergraduate studies at IMM Graduate School, was quoted by Daily Maverick saying that the Black Friday scorecard in 2026 needs to be measured well past 27 November.

That makes sense for most businesses. A clinic that promoted a new service this week may get the bookings in two weeks. An advocate who posted a helpful explainer may get the call in January. A retailer may see the sale on payday, not on Friday.

If you judge the week by Friday night, you will either panic or celebrate too early. Both lead to bad decisions for next year.

What numbers should a business count after a campaign?

Four numbers, counted the same way every week. Here is the scorecard. Fill it in for a normal week in early November first, so you have something fair to compare with.

NumberWhere to find itWhat it tells you
EnquiriesWhatsApp chats, DMs, calls, forms, walk-ins who mention a postDid the marketing make people reach out?
RepliesHow many of those enquiries got an answer the same dayDid interest go cold before anyone answered?
New customersBookings, orders or signed clients from those enquiriesDid the right people come, or just curious ones?
Money inYour till, bank or invoicing for the same periodWas it worth what you spent?

Likes and views still have a place. They tell you if people noticed. But they sit at the top of the page, not the bottom line.

How do you fill in the scorecard this week?

It takes about twenty minutes a week. No new software.

1. Pick your normal week. Choose a week in early November with no specials. Count the four numbers for it. That is your baseline.

2. Ask every new customer one question. "Where did you hear about us?" Ask it in store, on the phone and in the chat. Write the answer next to the sale. This one habit closes the gap between the post and the till.

3. Count on three dates. Count once for Black Friday week, again around 15 December, and again in the second week of January. Same four numbers, same way, every time.

4. Put the money next to the spend. Write down what you spent on ads and specials. Then write down the money in. If you ran a discount, count what you kept, not just what you sold.

5. Write three lines. What we did. What happened. What we will do next. If you cannot write the second line, you know what to fix before the next campaign.

This is exactly what Page Management covers every month: a plain-language report on what we did, what happened and what is next.

Why does this matter more now?

Because every ad costs more. Meta's own results for the third quarter of 2026 show the average price per ad up 14% year over year. When each ad costs more, guessing costs more too.

Discounts make the maths sharper. Hiten Keshave, CEO of Unconventional Capital & Advisory, was quoted by Daily Maverick saying that a 20% discount on a product with a 40% gross profit margin forces a small business to double its unit sales just to stand still. A busy Black Friday is not always a good one. Only the scorecard tells you which one you had.

Here is a simple example. The figures are illustrative. Two businesses each spend on ads this week. One gets 400 likes and 6 enquiries. The other gets 90 likes and 21 enquiries, 9 of which book. On likes, the first one wins. On money, it is not close.

What does a good marketing report look like?

It shows numbers that grow month by month, with a short note on why. Here is a real example from our team's past work. For a legal professional's personal brand, the first three months on Facebook (July to September 2025) looked like this:

MonthPostsClicksShares
July 2025602,37529
August 2025883,65845

That is 268,555 views on 245 posts in three months. But the point is not the big number. The point is that every month had a line for what was done, what happened and what came next. That is how you know what to keep and what to stop.

Your report does not need to be fancy. It needs to be honest, regular and about the numbers that matter to you: enquiries, replies and how many became customers.

Questions people are asking

How do I know if my Black Friday marketing worked?

Count enquiries, replies, new customers and money in for Black Friday week, and compare them with a normal week in early November. Ask every new customer where they heard about you, so you can link sales back to your posts and ads.

How long after Black Friday should I measure the results?

Keep measuring until at least mid-December and ideally into January. Many people look online first and buy later, so the sales from this week's marketing can arrive weeks after it.

Why do my likes not match my sales?

Likes show that people noticed a post, not that they bought. Sales often happen somewhere else, such as in store, on the phone or on WhatsApp, days later. Ask customers where they heard about you to connect the two.

What should a monthly marketing report include?

Three short parts: what was done, what happened and what is next. The numbers should include enquiries, replies, new customers and money in, compared with the month before, not only likes and views.
