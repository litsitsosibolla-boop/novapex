Universities Are Full. Your Admissions Inbox Is Next.

Published 2 October 20266 min read Novapex

Public TVET colleges closed applications for 2027 on 30 September. For the 2026 intake, more than 100,000 students with a bachelor's pass had no public university seat to go to. Many of those families now message private colleges. The colleges that reply the same day, ask one useful question and offer a booked next step will enrol them.

Every year the same thing happens. Public universities fill up, the application windows close, and thousands of students and parents start looking for a plan B. They do not fill in long forms. They send a WhatsApp, a DM or a one-line email: "Do you still have space for 2027?"

For a private college, tutoring business or training provider, that message is the most valuable thing that will arrive all year. And many of them go quiet, not because the student lost interest, but because nobody answered well enough, fast enough.

Why are private colleges about to get more inquiries?

Because the public options are closing one after another, and the demand is far bigger than the seats.

Applications for the 2027 academic year at public TVET colleges were open until 30 September 2026, according to the Department of Higher Education and Training. NSFAS opened its 2027 funding cycle in September and says it received 51,474 applications in the first four days. NSFAS says the window closes on 31 October. Matric finals start on 13 October and run to 26 November.

Put together, this is the season when families make decisions. Some missed a deadline. Some expect a rejection. Some want a backup before results come out. All of them are shopping, and many of them are shopping in chat.

How many students miss out on a public university place?

More than 100,000 qualifying students a year, on the latest figures. Daily Maverick reported in January 2026 that South Africa recorded more than 340,000 bachelor's passes against only about 235,000 spaces at public universities.

The gap at individual universities is even starker:

Private higher education is already a big part of the answer. DHET's latest statistics show 138 private higher education institutions in 2023, with 286,454 students, or 21.1% of all higher education enrolments.

So the demand is real. The question is which college catches it.

The college with the best reply wins, not the college with the best brochure.

How fast should a private college reply to a student inquiry?

The same working day, and within the hour if you can. A student who messages four colleges on a Sunday evening will take the first clear answer seriously and treat the rest as backups.

Do not assume you are fast. A mystery-shopping study of 54 UK universities by UniQuest and Edified, reported by ICEF Monitor, found that one in four inquiries received no response at all, and only one in six got a follow-up. There is no published South African study like it. Until you have measured your own inbox, assume it is no faster.

This is the Response stage of our method, and in enrolment season it carries most of the weight. Marketing has already done its job. The inquiry is in your inbox.

How do you tell a serious applicant from a browsing parent?

Ask one question in your first reply that sorts them. Not a form. One question.

  • "Which programme are you looking at, and for which intake?" A clear answer means a real plan.
  • "Have you already applied to a university this year?" This tells you whether you are the first choice or the backup, and how to talk to them.
  • "Who will be paying, you or a parent or guardian?" The decision maker is often not the person typing.

Then tag every conversation hot, warm or not now. Hot means a named programme, a named intake and a decision maker in the chat. Those get a call the same day.

What should a first reply to a student inquiry say?

The answer, one question, and a next step with a time. Here is an example of what that looks like. It is illustrative, so adapt it to your programmes.

The weak reply is polite. It is also a dead end. The parent now has to do the work, and the next college will do it for them.

How do you stop enrolment inquiries going quiet?

Plan the follow-up before the rush, not during it. Five things to set up this week:

1. Name an owner for every channel. WhatsApp, Instagram DMs, the website form and the main email address. If nobody owns it, nobody answers it on a Saturday.

2. Write your five answers now. Space, fees, entry requirements, funding options and start dates cover most first questions. Have a clear, honest answer ready for each.

3. Use one sorting question. Pick one from the list above and put it in every first reply.

4. Follow up three times. Day one, day three and the day after matric results. Each message adds something new, such as a fee breakdown, an open day date or a funding tip. Never just "checking in".

5. Count one number weekly. Inquiries that became a booked call or campus visit. Not total inquiries. If inquiries rise and booked calls do not, the leak is in your replies.

The universities' capacity problem is your enrolment opportunity, but only if your inbox is ready for it. The families are already typing. The question is whether anyone answers.
