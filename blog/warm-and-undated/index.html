The Meeting Ended Warm and Undated

19 August 20263 min read Novapex

The call went well. They were engaged, they asked good questions, they said this is exactly the sort of thing we have been meaning to sort out. Everybody hung up pleased.

Nobody agreed what happens next.

That deal is already lost. It will take six weeks to admit it, and during those six weeks it will sit in the pipeline as a live opportunity, distorting the forecast and absorbing follow-up that would be better spent elsewhere.

Why warmth is not progress

A good conversation feels like movement because it is pleasant, and pleasant is easy to mistake for productive. But nothing structural changed. The buyer left with the same problem they arrived with, plus a favourable impression that will decay at roughly the speed of their inbox.

The moment the call ends is the highest their intent will ever be. Everything after that point is decay. A next step agreed while intent is at its peak converts. The same next step proposed nine days later, by email, arrives to a person whose urgent problem has been quietly reclassified as a someday problem.

This is why "I will send something over and we will take it from there" is not a next step. It is a polite way of ending a conversation without deciding anything, and both parties usually know it.

The fix is one sentence

End every first conversation with a named action, an owner and a date. Out loud, on the call, before anyone hangs up.

"So I will send the scope by Thursday, and can we put twenty minutes in for Monday at ten to go through it?"

That is the whole intervention. It is not a technique and there is nothing clever in it. It works because it converts a feeling into a commitment while the feeling is still strong enough to produce one.

The objection to this is always the same: it feels pushy. It is worth noticing that it only feels pushy to you. From the buyer's side, someone who ends a conversation by saying precisely what will happen and when is easier to deal with than someone who leaves it vague. Vagueness is not politeness. It is a cost you are passing to them.

The number that shows you have this problem

Count the opportunities in your pipeline right now with no scheduled next action. Not ones with an overdue action, ones with no action at all.

Then count how many of those went quiet after a conversation everybody described as positive.

If the second number is most of the first, you do not have a closing problem or a pricing problem. You have a diary problem, and it is the cheapest thing on this entire list to fix.

Do this today

Take your next five first conversations. End each one by proposing a specific action on a specific date. Write down whether they agreed on the call.

Then compare those five, six weeks from now, against the five before them.
