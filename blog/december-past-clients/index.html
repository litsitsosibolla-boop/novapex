They Booked You Last Year and Went Quiet. Here Is How to Tell Them About December Without Sounding Like Spam.

Published 10 November 20267 min read Novapex

To tell past clients about your December slots without sounding like spam, send each one a short personal message, not a broadcast. Use their name, mention what they booked before, give your real December dates, and end with one easy question. Follow up once if they go quiet, then stop.

Scroll back through your WhatsApp. Past the new chats, past last month. There they are: people who booked you last year, paid, said thank you, and then went quiet.

Nobody said no. Life just moved on. And now December is coming, your diary has gaps, and you are posting on Instagram hoping someone new will message.

The people most likely to book you this December already know your work. They already trust you. They are sitting in your chats. Most owners never message them, because it feels pushy.

It does not have to be. Here is how to do it so it feels like a favour, not an ad.

Why should I message past clients before December?

Because a past client is the easiest booking you will get all year. They know your price, your quality and where you are. You do not have to win their trust again. You only have to remind them you exist before someone else does.

Returning customers are where much of small-business money comes from. In a sponsored Daily Maverick article published on 1 July 2026, the payments company Yoco said that, by its own figures, "last quarter alone, R4.9 billion in sales across the Yoco merchant network came from returning customers."

December makes this urgent. Your past clients are planning their month now. If you are not in their chat, they will book whoever is.

How do I message past clients without it feeling like spam?

Make it personal, make it short, and make it easy to say no. Spam is the same message, sent to everyone, again and again. A good message is the opposite on all three.

People notice the difference. In its 2025 Consumer Marketing Fatigue Report, the marketing software company Optimove surveyed 329 people in the United States. 78% of them said they "prefer fewer but more targeted messages", and 89% said they unsubscribe from brands that bombard them with repetitive offers. That is a US study, but you have felt the same thing on your own phone.

One personal message to someone who already paid you is worth more than a week of posts to strangers.

What should the message say?

Four parts, in this order:

  1. Their name and what they had before. "Hi Thandi! Hope the braids lasted well." This shows you remember them, and that this message is for them only.
  2. The news, in one line. Your December dates are open, and how many you really have.
  3. A reason to answer now. First pick for past clients, a real cut-off date, or a set you know they liked. Never a fake "only 2 left".
  4. One easy question. Something they can answer in a word. "Shall I keep one for you?"

Should I use a broadcast list or send one by one?

Send one by one. A broadcast looks like a broadcast, and people treat it like one. Twenty personal messages take you about half an hour. That is less time than one Instagram post, and each one lands in a chat where the client already knows you.

If you have more than twenty past clients, start with the ones who booked most recently or most often. Do the rest the next day.

What if they read it and do not reply?

Follow up once, two or three days later, with something new: a slot that opened, a date that is filling up, or a simple check. For example: "Hi Thandi, just a quick one: I'm holding a Saturday morning for past clients until the weekend, then I'll open it up. Want it?"

If they still do not answer, stop. Leave the door open for next time with a kind last line, such as: "No stress if December is full for you. I'll be here in the new year." They will remember you were easy to deal with.

Give people an easy way out from the start, too. "Just say if you'd rather I didn't message about bookings" costs you nothing and makes you look confident, not desperate.

How do I find my past clients in WhatsApp?

Set it up once, in about fifteen minutes:

  1. Add a label in WhatsApp Business called "Past client".
  2. Scroll back through the last twelve months of chats. Label everyone who paid you.
  3. Write your message once and save it as a quick reply, with gaps for the name and what they had.
  4. Add a second label called "Asked about December" for everyone who replies, so nobody gets lost in the rush.

Questions owners ask

Is it pushy to message past clients about bookings?

Not if it is personal and easy to say no to. Use their name, mention what they had before, ask one question and follow up only once. Pushy is the same message sent to everyone, again and again.

When should I message past clients about December?

As early as you can, while they are still planning. Past clients who hear from you first can book before your diary fills, and before someone else messages them.

Should I offer past clients a discount?

You do not need to. First pick of your December dates, a real cut-off or a set they liked is often enough. If you do offer something, make it real and give it an end date.

How many times should I follow up with a past client?

Once. Send one follow-up two or three days later with something new, such as a slot that opened. If they still do not reply, stop and leave the door open for next time.
