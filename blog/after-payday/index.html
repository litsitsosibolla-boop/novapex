They Said “I’ll Book After Payday.” Here Is How to Follow Up So They Actually Do.

Published 17 November 20267 min read Novapex

When a client says they will book after payday, do not reply "No problem, let me know." Ask which payday, the 25th or month end, and say you will message them that morning. Offer to pencil in a slot until then. Label the chat with the date, send one short message on payday, follow up once, then stop.

They loved the work. They asked the price. They said yes. Then: "Let me book after payday."

You reply "No problem, just let me know." Payday comes and goes. They never message. You never chase, because you do not want to seem pushy about money.

That chat was not lost to price. It was lost to a gap of a week or two that nobody owned.

Here is how to own it, without being pushy.

Is "after payday" a real yes or a polite no?

Most of the time it is a real yes with a timing problem. They want it, and the money is not in their account yet. That is normal at the end of the month.

Money is tight. The FNB/BER Consumer Confidence Index fell from -7 in the first quarter of 2026 to -19 in the second quarter, as reported by FAnews on 20 July 2026. When people are careful, they wait for payday before they commit. They still mean it.

The problem is what happens next. By payday, they have a dozen other bills and other messages. If you are not in their chat that day, the booking slips to next month, or to someone who was.

"After payday" is a booking with a date on it. Treat it like one.

What should I reply when a client says they will book after payday?

Agree warmly, then put a date on it. Your reply should do three things: make it easy, ask which day they are paid, and say you will message them.

Every reply on the right ends with a question and moves the next step to you. "Let me know" leaves it with them. They will not let you know. Not because they are rude, but because they are busy.

Should I hold a slot for them until payday?

Yes, if you can, but put a time limit on it. A pencilled-in slot gives them a reason to come back. A slot held forever costs you a booking.

Say it plainly: "I'll hold the 29th for you until the evening of the 26th. If you can't make it, no stress, I'll open it up." That is honest, it is kind, and it creates a real reason to reply. Never invent one. If you have plenty of space, do not pretend you do not.

If they want to lock it in now, offer a small deposit to hold the date and the rest on the day. Some clients will take that, and the chat stops being a maybe.

How do I remember who said "after payday"?

Use a label, not your memory. In WhatsApp Business, set it up once in about five minutes:

  1. Create a label called "After payday".
  2. Add the date to the chat. Pin the chat or write the date in the label name, for example "Payday 25th" and "Payday month end".
  3. Set one phone reminder for the morning of each payday: "Send payday messages."
  4. Save your payday message as a quick reply, so it takes ten seconds to send.

On the morning of payday, open the label and send each message by hand, with their name. It takes a few minutes, and these are the warmest chats you have.

What do I send on payday?

Short, friendly, and with your real times in it. No guilt, no "just checking in". Remind them what they wanted and give them an easy choice.

For example (made-up name and times): "Hi Lerato! Happy payday! You wanted to book the lash fill. I have the 28th at 10am or the 29th at 2pm. Which one should I put you down for?"

Two options, one question. They can answer in one word.

What if they still do not reply after payday?

Follow up once, two or three days later, with something new: a time that opened, or the slot you were holding. For example: "Hi Lerato, I'm keeping the 29th at 2pm for you until tonight, then I'll open it up. Still keen?"

If they stay quiet, stop. Close the loop with a kind last line: "No stress if this month is tight. Message me whenever you're ready and I'll find you a slot." They will remember you were easy to deal with, and many come back the next month.

Why does this matter more in December?

Because December pay arrives early and January pay is a long way off. Standard Bank's analysis, published in December 2024, found the gap between the December and January paydays is "42 days for those usually paid on the 25th and 49 days for month-end earners."

So "after payday" in December can mean two things. Some clients will book the moment December pay lands, before it is spent. Others will push to January. Ask which, and label it. A client who says "after January payday" is not lost. They are booked for January, if you message them.

How do I know if it is working?

Count it. Judge it on numbers from your own inbox, not on how busy you feel.

Questions owners ask

Is it rude to follow up when a client said they will book after payday?

No. They told you when they would be ready, so a message on that day is helpful, not pushy. Keep it short, use their name, give two times and ask one question.

When should I message a client who said after payday?

On the morning of their payday. Ask at the start whether they are paid on the 25th or at month end, then message that morning while the money is still there.

Should I hold a booking for someone until payday?

Yes, if you can, but give it a clear end time. Pencil the slot in until a day or two after payday, tell them when you will open it up, and keep to it.

How many times should I follow up after payday?

Once. Send your payday message, then one follow-up two or three days later with something new. If they still do not reply, close the loop kindly and stop.
