WhatsApp Just Put a Price on Your Empty Replies

Published 29 September 20266 min read Novapex

From 1 October 2026, Meta charges South African businesses for every reply they send on the WhatsApp Business Platform, at roughly 12 cents a message. The fee is small. The real cost is what it exposes: most chatbot flows send several messages that say nothing before a buyer gets a real answer.

On 1 October, a free thing stops being free. For almost two years, replying to a customer on WhatsApp inside the 24-hour window cost a business nothing. That is over, and most companies will treat it as a line item for finance to absorb.

That would be a mistake. Not because the fee is large. Because the fee is the first time anyone has put a number on how many of your replies are empty.

What exactly changes on 1 October?

Meta will charge per message for service messages. These are the free-form replies a business sends, by a person or a bot, inside the 24-hour window after a customer writes in. Meta says these have not been charged since 1 November 2024.

Three things to know:

  • It is the Platform, not the app. The change hits businesses using the WhatsApp Business Platform, the API behind chatbots, CRMs and messaging providers. The free WhatsApp Business app on a phone is not affected.
  • It is billed in dollars. MyBroadband reported roughly 12 cents ($0.0076) per reply. Helm, a local messaging provider, puts its working estimate at 10 to 15 cents. The rand amount will move with the exchange rate.
  • Every message counts. Not every conversation. Every message.

That last point is the one that matters.

Why is the fee a Response problem, not a finance problem?

Because the bill is a count of your messages, and most flows are padded with messages that move nothing forward.

Open your own WhatsApp line and send it a real question, the kind a buyer would ask. Then count what comes back before a useful answer arrives. In most flows it looks something like this: a greeting, a line about the business, a menu, a request to pick a number, a confirmation, a promise that someone will be in touch.

Six messages. None of them answered the question. From 1 October, each one is billed.

We have written before that a fast, empty reply is worse than a slow, good one, because it spends the buyer's attention without giving anything back. Until now, that cost was invisible. It showed up as buyers going quiet, which looks like nothing at all in your data. Now part of it shows up on an invoice.

R1,440 a month will not sink anyone. That is the trap. The number is small enough to ignore, so the flow stays the same, and the flow is the expensive part.

The 2026 South African Customer Experience Report found that 81% of consumers had a negative customer experience, up from 76% in 2024. Only 24% posted about it publicly, down from 50% in 2023. Buyers are not complaining. They are leaving quietly. A padded WhatsApp flow is exactly the kind of experience people walk away from without saying a word.

The fee is cheap. The buyers who give up on your menu are not.

What should a good WhatsApp reply contain?

The same three things as any good first reply, in this order:

  • The answer to what they asked. Not a confirmation that they asked.
  • One question that moves it forward. Something that qualifies or clarifies, answerable in a sentence.
  • A next step with a time attached. "Can we call you at 10:00 or 14:00 tomorrow?" beats "Someone will be in touch."

Put all three in one message where you can. That single message is cheaper than six and far more likely to start a real conversation.

How do you fix a padded flow this week?

You do not need new software. You need an hour and your last fifty conversations.

1. Count. Pick fifty recent WhatsApp conversations. For each, count the business messages sent before the buyer got a real answer. Write down the average.

2. Cut. Merge the greeting, the introduction and the menu into one message. Delete any message that only confirms something the buyer can already see.

3. Answer early. Look at what people actually ask. It is rarely more than five things. Write a real answer to each and let the bot, or the person, send it in the first reply.

4. Hand over sooner. If a buyer shows real intent, a named person should take over in the next message, not after a qualifying quiz. A bot that asks six questions before finding out whether someone is serious now costs money on every turn.

5. Measure one number weekly. Billed messages per conversation that reached a real call, meeting or quote. If it falls and conversations hold steady or rise, the fix is working.

Where does this fit in the bigger picture?

This sits at the Response stage of our method. Response asks one question: how fast is the first reply, and does it move anything forward? The WhatsApp fee does not change the answer. It just makes the second half of that question show up on a bill.

If your WhatsApp flow is tight, the new charge is a rounding error. If it is padded, Meta has just handed you a free audit. Count the messages, and you will find out how many of your replies were ever doing any work.

Questions people are asking

When does WhatsApp start charging for service replies in South Africa?

From 1 October 2026. Meta will charge per message for service messages, the free-form replies a business sends inside the 24-hour window after a customer writes in. These replies have been free since 1 November 2024.

How much does a WhatsApp service reply cost in South Africa?

Meta bills in US dollars. MyBroadband reported roughly 12 cents (about $0.0076) per reply. Helm estimates 10 to 15 cents. Check your provider's current rate card before budgeting.

Does the new WhatsApp charge affect the free WhatsApp Business app?

No. The change applies to the WhatsApp Business Platform, the API that chatbots, CRMs and messaging providers run on. The free WhatsApp Business app on a phone is not affected.

How do I reduce my WhatsApp Business costs without losing leads?

Cut the messages that say nothing. Merge greetings and menus into one reply, answer the question in the first message, ask one question that qualifies, and hand serious buyers to a person early. Fewer, better messages cost less and convert more.
