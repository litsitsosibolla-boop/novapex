The Post-and-Pray Problem

12 August 20264 min read The Novapex Brief

Walk into almost any small or mid-size business, ask to see the marketing plan, and what you'll usually find isn't a plan at all. It's a person, often the owner, posting whenever they remember to, hoping something lands, and calling it strategy because there's nothing else to call it.

This is post-and-pray. It's the default state of marketing for most businesses in this region right now, and not because the people running them aren't sharp. It's because nobody ever showed them what the alternative actually looks like in practice.

Why it feels like it's working, even when it isn't

Post-and-pray survives because it occasionally produces a good week. A post does well, a product sells out, and that one good week gets remembered as proof the approach works. The bad weeks, the missed messages, the content nobody planned, those get quietly forgotten, because there's no system tracking them either.

The real cost isn't that nothing works. It's that nothing gets learned, because nothing is being measured on purpose.

A business can run like this for years. It will even grow, slowly, on the strength of the owner's own energy. What it can't do is grow without them, because the entire marketing function lives in one person's memory.

What a system actually replaces it with

A real content system means knowing what's going out next week before this week is over. It means a saved reply for the question you answer every single day. It means a simple, honest way to know which post led to a sale, not just which one got the most likes.

None of that requires new technology or a bigger budget. It requires someone to sit down and build the system once, properly, instead of posting into the void one more time and hoping this is the week it clicks.

The test

Here's the quickest way to know which side of the line you're on. Ask yourself what you're publishing eight days from now. If the answer is a specific thing you could hand to someone else to execute, you have a system. If the answer is “we'll see what comes up,” you have a habit, and habits don't scale.
