You are between clients. Your phone shows six new chats.

"How much for a full set?" "Are you available this week?" "Just looking for now." "Can you send more info?" "Any specials?"

You have answered every one of these a hundred times. You still type each answer from scratch, fast, with one thumb. And the fast version is usually the one that ends the chat.

This post is about the part of the Reply Kit built for that moment: the First-Reply Scripts. What they cover, why they work, what the Kit costs and who should not buy it.

Why does the first reply decide if they book?

Because the first reply sets the direction of the whole chat. If it answers and stops, the buyer has nothing easy to say back. If it answers and asks one clear question, the chat keeps moving toward a date.

For most owners, that first reply happens in WhatsApp. Standard Bank's Township Informal Economy Report found that 74% of small and medium enterprises use WhatsApp to reach customers, while Instagram is used by 47% (as reported by AllAfrica on 20 October 2025).

So your inbox is not a side channel. It is where the sale is won or lost, one reply at a time.

A first reply that answers and stops is a goodbye. A first reply that answers and asks is the start of a booking.

Most first messages fall into five types. The First-Reply Scripts cover each of them. Here is what each one usually means, and what your reply has to do.

The messageWhat it usually meansWhat your reply must do
"How much?""Is this in my budget, and is it worth it?"Give the price, what it includes, and a next step
"Is it available?""Can I get in soon, or should I look elsewhere?"Offer two clear options so they only have to pick one

What does a good first reply look like?

Every good first reply has the same three parts: a short answer, one useful detail, and one easy question. Here are two examples, written for this post to show the pattern. The names and prices are made up.

The reply that ends the chatThe reply that keeps it going
"Yes we have space.""Hi Thandi! Yes, I have a 3pm or an 11am slot open this week. A full set takes about 90 minutes. Which time suits you?"
"Sure, here is our price list." (sends a PDF)"Happy to help, Sipho! Most clients start with the one-hour session at R450. Are you after a one-off session or something regular?"

The first column answers the question. The second answers it and hands the buyer an easy next message. That is the only rule: no first reply ends without a question.

The Reply Kit has 40 first replies built this way, one for each kind of message you get.

Why not just write my own first replies?

You can, and you should know the pattern either way. The problem is not knowing what to say. It is saying it well forty times a week, between clients, from memory.

When you are rushed, you type the shortest answer. The shortest answer almost never has a question in it. Ready replies fix that, because the good version is already written and only takes two taps to send.

Every lost inquiry also costs more than it looks. In Xero's 2026 survey of 427 South African small businesses, 62% said they had cash flow issues over the past year. A buyer who messaged you already cost you a post, an ad or a referral. Losing them at the first reply is money you already spent.
