They Asked “How Much?” and Vanished. Here Is What to Send Instead.

Published 13 October 20266 min read Novapex

When a client asks "how much?" on WhatsApp, send the price in your first reply, say in one line what it includes, and end with one easy question that moves toward a booking. A bare price gives them nothing to answer, so they go quiet. With money tight, the price question now comes first.

You know the chat. Someone finds you on Instagram. They message: "Hi, how much for a full set?" You reply between clients: "R450." Two blue ticks. Then nothing.

You tell yourself they were just shopping around. Some were. But many of them wanted to book. Your reply just gave them nowhere to go.

Why do clients ask "how much?" and then vanish?

Because a price on its own ends the conversation. It answers their question and asks nothing back, so the easiest thing for them to do is nothing.

And right now, more people are leading with the price. Household budgets are under pressure. IOL reported on 6 October 2026 that the PMBEJD Household Affordability Index put the average Household Food Basket at R5,488.06 in September, up R108.64, or 2%, on a year earlier. Of the 44 basic foods it tracks, 21 went up in price during the month.

The same report describes shoppers who compare prices and question whether paying more is worth it. Encore's managing director told IOL that shoppers are not simply becoming more price conscious, they are becoming more value conscious. That is the person in your DMs. They are not only asking what it costs. They are asking whether it is worth it, and your reply has to answer both.

The other thing to know: unhappy clients rarely tell you. Everlytic, citing the 2026 South African Customer Experience Report (2,000 consumers), says that rather than complain publicly, 52% of South African consumers will simply stop supporting a brand entirely, and 55% will quietly switch to a competitor. Silence after your price is not always "no". Often it is "not sure", and nobody followed up.

Should I send the price straight away or ask questions first?

Send the price straight away. If you dodge with "it depends, what are you looking for?", many people read it as a trap and leave before you get to the number.

If your price really does depend on details, give a range and ask the one question that narrows it. For example (made-up prices): "Event catering is R180 to R320 a head, depending on the menu. How many guests are you expecting?" They get a number and a reason to reply in the same message.

What does a good price reply look like?

Three parts, in this order: the price, what it includes, one question. The question should be easy to answer and point toward a booking: a date, a choice between two options, or the one detail you need.

Here are three examples. The businesses and prices are made up, to show the shape.

BusinessThe reply that ends the chatThe reply that keeps it going
Lash tech"R450.""A full classic set is R450 and takes about two hours. It lasts three to four weeks with a fill. Would a morning or an afternoon suit you better?"
Wedding photographer"Packages start at R15,000.""Full-day coverage is R15,000, with your edited photos in an online gallery. What is your wedding date? I will check if I am free."
Maths tutor"R300 per hour.""One-on-one maths is R300 an hour, at home or online. Which topic is giving your child the most trouble right now?"

Look at what changed. The price is still there, in the first line, with no dodging. But now the client knows what they are paying for, and they have an easy next step. Answering "morning" takes two seconds. Saying nothing now feels like leaving a question hanging.

A price on its own is a full stop. A price with a question after it is a conversation.

How do I make my price feel worth it?

Say what they get, not what you do. "Two hours" and "lasts three to four weeks" mean more to a client than "classic technique". "Edited photos in an online gallery" means more than "professional editing".

Keep it to one line. You are not writing a brochure. You are giving a busy person just enough to feel the price is fair.

Three quick checks before you send:

1. Is the price in the first line? Not at the end of a paragraph. Not in a PDF they have to open.

2. Is there one thing they get, in plain words? Time, result, or what is included.

3. Does it end with a question mark? If it ends with a full stop, rewrite the last line.

What do I say if they go quiet after the price?

Send one light follow-up the next day. Make it easy to say yes, and easy to say no. For example: "Hi Naledi, just checking in. Should I keep a spot open for you this week? No stress if the timing is not right."

That last line matters. It gives them a polite way out, which often makes it easier for them to reply at all. A "not right now" is still useful. It tells you to try again later instead of guessing.

Do not send "???" or "Hello?" or a voice note asking why they did not reply. Those make the client feel chased, and chased people block.

How do I know if it is working?

Count it. Judge any change on three numbers from your own inbox, not on how the reply feels.

NumberHow to count it
Reply timeHow long before your first answer, on average

Close rateBookings divided by everyone who asked the price

Take this week as your starting point. Write down how many people asked "how much?" and how many booked. Then use the three-part reply for a week and count again. Your own numbers will tell you more than any article.

WhatsApp is where many of these chats happen. In the MEF Consumer Survey 2026, 40% of South African respondents chose WhatsApp to start contact with companies, second only to email at 70% and ahead of phone calls at 36%. For many clients, your price reply is the first real conversation they have with your business.

Questions owners ask

Should I put my prices on Instagram so people stop asking?

You can, and some owners do. People will still message to ask, because they want to check it applies to them. Treat every "how much?" as a buyer raising a hand, and answer with the price, what it includes and one question.

What if my price is higher than other businesses?

Say it anyway, then say what they get for it in one line. Shoppers are comparing prices more, so a clear reason to pay more matters. Hiding the price until later does not make it smaller.

How long should I wait before following up after sending a price?

About a day. Send one light message that makes it easy to say yes or no. If there is still no reply, try once more a few days later, then stop.

Should I send my price list as a PDF?

Not as your first reply. Answer the question they asked in the chat, in plain words. Send a full list afterwards if they want to compare options.
