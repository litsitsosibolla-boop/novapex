More Content Will Not Fix a Leak

5 August 20263 min read Litsitso Sibolla

Say a hundred inquiries reach you in a month and you convert eight of them. The instinct, almost universally, is to go and find another hundred.

Work out what that actually buys.

The arithmetic nobody does

Doubling inquiries at the same conversion rate takes you from eight customers to sixteen. To get there you have doubled the spend, doubled the volume of work landing on the same team, and left every existing leak exactly where it was, now leaking twice as fast.

Now take the other route. Leave volume alone and move conversion from eight percent to twelve. That is not a heroic number. It is what closing one genuine gap in the system usually produces. You get twelve customers instead of eight, a fifty percent increase, at no additional acquisition cost and with no additional load on anyone.

Then, and only then, double the volume. Now you are at twenty-four rather than sixteen, from the same doubled spend.

Same money. A third more revenue. The only difference is the order the two moves were made in.

Why the order gets reversed

Because more attention is legible and fewer leaks is not.

A campaign has a launch date, a budget line and a chart. It looks like marketing happening. Fixing the reason inquiries die three days after arriving has no launch, no chart and nothing to present. It is a change to how people work, which is why it is usually nobody's job and why it is still available in almost every business.

There is a second reason, less comfortable. Volume is somebody else's problem to solve. You can buy it. Conversion is your problem, and fixing it means looking closely at how your own people currently work, which is a harder meeting than approving a budget.

When more volume is genuinely the right answer

Sometimes it is, and it is worth being straight about when.

If your system is tight, meaning inquiries are captured properly, answered quickly, qualified deliberately and closed on a date, then conversion is not where your next increment comes from. Buy attention. That is what it is for.

The problem is that almost every company believes it is in that position, and very few have checked. The check is not difficult. Take your last forty inquiries and follow each one through to its outcome. Not a sample you choose, the last forty in order.

You will find one of two things. Either they moved through a system, in which case spend the money on demand with a clear conscience. Or they moved through four different improvised paths depending on who happened to pick them up, in which case you have found something more valuable than a new channel.

One thing to do this week

Before the next budget conversation, work out your own version of the arithmetic above. Your inquiry volume, your conversion rate, your average deal value. Then model the two routes: double the volume, or move conversion by four points.

Take both numbers into the meeting. The conversation changes when the alternative is on the page.
