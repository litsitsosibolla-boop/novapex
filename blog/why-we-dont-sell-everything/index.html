Why We Don't Sell What Everyone Else Is Selling

15 July 20265 min read The Novapex Brief

There's a version of this studio that would sell a great deal more than we currently do. It would sell AI agents running your entire marketing function. Programmatic ad buying across streaming TV. Cross-channel attribution modelling. Shoppable livestream commerce. Every headline from the latest global trends report, repackaged with a local price tag.

We deliberately don't. Not because those things aren't real, several of them are genuinely transformative in markets that are ready for them, but because most businesses here aren't, and selling someone infrastructure they can't use is just an expensive way to waste their year.

The two bad options

Most local businesses are stuck choosing between them. Option one is post-and-pray: no strategy, no system, content going out and hoping something sticks. Option two is someone who read a global trends report and now wants to sell a Fortune 500 tech stack to a shop that doesn't have the data to feed it.

Both fail, for opposite reasons. The first never builds anything. The second builds something nobody can operate after the consultant leaves.

What we hold back, and why

Fully AI-run marketing needs data systems most businesses here are years from having. Automated ad buying across streaming and retail networks needs an ad-tech backbone that doesn't exist in this market yet. Heavy cross-channel measurement modelling needs years of clean historical data almost nobody has collected. Shoppable livestream commerce needs payment rails and creator infrastructure that aren't mature here.

We watch all of it. We just don't sell it until it's genuinely usable here. That's the difference between staying current and chasing trends.

What we import instead

Plenty of what works in bigger markets transfers today, cheaply. Systems over tactics. Fixing what happens after someone shows up, rather than buying more people to show up. Owning the relationship. A real list, a real catalogue, a real record of who's interested, instead of renting it from an algorithm. Small engaged communities over large dead ones. Recognisable content series instead of one-off posts. Smaller trusted creators over expensive names. And a real human voice, at a moment when audiences have started actively distrusting content that obviously came from a machine.

Every one of those is buildable in this market right now, with tools that already exist. That's the whole bet: internationally credible thinking, applied only where it can actually be used.

The discipline is the pitch

Saying no to revenue is uncomfortable. It's also the clearest signal we can give you about how we'll behave once you're paying us, because a studio willing to sell you something you can't use today will happily do it again next year.
